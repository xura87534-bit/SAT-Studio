    How the radar works (and how to get accurate results)

    Speed is distance divided by time: v = d / t. You mark two lines across the path and measure the real distance d between them, for example with a tape measure, a known number of floor tiles or road markings. The app learns what the empty scene looks like, notices when something moving reaches line A and then line B, and takes t as the time between those two moments, using the time each frame was captured and interpolating between frames. Then it divides.

    Example: A and B are 10 m apart and a car takes 0.72 s: v = 10 ÷ 0.72 = 13.9 m/s = 50 km/h (× 3.6 converts m/s to km/h).

    Accuracy. The camera sends roughly 10–20 pictures a second, so each crossing time can be off by up to one frame. The app shows that as a range (for example 48–52 km/h). To make the range smaller:

    • Put the lines further apart: a larger d means a larger t, and the same frame error matters less.
    • Film from the side, so the object crosses the picture rather than coming towards the camera.
    • Put each line exactly where your measured marks are on the ground, in the lane the object uses. Perspective doesn’t matter as long as the lines sit on the real marks.
    • Keep the camera still and the scene evenly lit. Lower the sensitivity if leaves or shadows trigger it, raise it for small or dark objects.
    • For a recorded video, play it at ¼× (Setup → Video file): timing comes from the video’s own timestamps, so slow playback gives the detector every frame.

    This is a learning and hobby tool, not a certified speed-enforcement device.

    Recordings on the camera’s memory card

    The camera records to its TF (microSD) card in one-minute files. Live video and recording pause while a file downloads.

    Picture source

    The radar works with any source, so you can also measure with your phone’s camera or a video you filmed.

    Connecting your camera
    1. Switch the camera on. If it was set up with the V720 app before, hold the reset button (side, next to the clip) for 5 seconds until the blue light blinks fast. That puts it back into hotspot mode.
    2. On your MacBook, open Wi-Fi and join the camera’s own network. Its name usually starts with Nax_, MC- or the number on the sticker. There’s no internet on it, which is expected.
    3. On the Mac, run ./start-mac.command in the camera folder (or node server.js). It prints two addresses.
    4. Open the first address on the Mac. For the phone, join the same camera Wi-Fi and open the “On your phone” address.
    5. To install: on iPhone tap Share → Add to Home Screen; on Android / Chrome use Install app.

    Range is the camera’s Wi-Fi range (usually 10–30 m, more in open space). If the Mac is connected to the internet by cable or a second adapter, a VPN such as Tailscale lets you open the app from anywhere.
